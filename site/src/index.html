SeaCraft

Model a ship’s hull, then measure it: hydrostatics, stability, resistance, motions, lines plans and building patterns. Free software in Rust, with a desktop app, a command line and an MCP server. It opens and saves FREE!ship .fef files.

Results are not validated.

Most calculations have not been compared with tank tests, sea trials or approved software, and they can be wrong even when they look right. A PASS against a criterion is arithmetic, not an approval. Have a qualified person check anything you rely on. What has been checked, and the full disclaimer.

A hull is a control net. Pull one point and the surface follows.

SeaCraft models a hull the way FREE!ship does: a coarse net of points refined into a smooth subdivision surface, with creases where a chine or a stem needs a hard edge. There are no NURBS patches to keep in step. You work in four linked views, perspective, profile, plan and body plan, each labelled with the way it faces, and the hull is drawn on the graphics card so a large one still turns smoothly.

Pick points one by one, draw a rectangle round them, or take a whole station or lengthwise line, then drag them together. With connected editing on, a dragged point draws its neighbours along, fading with distance as a preset says. Hydrostatics update when you let go, undo goes back 64 steps, and autosave can bring your work back after a crash.

From lines to numbers, for the selected hull

The steps below follow one hull through the program. Every number comes from SeaCraft, and the commands get you the same numbers. Choosing another hull at the top of the page changes all of them.

  1. Cut the lines

    Stations, buttocks and waterlines are cut from the refined surface and exported as SVG, DXF or IGES. Curvature combs show you where a line is unfair.

  2. Measure the hydrostatics

    Volume, displacement, centres, coefficients and metacentres are integrated over the panels of the refined mesh, at any draft, trim and heel. The curve shows how the underwater volume is spread along the length.

  3. Check the stability

    Cross curves and righting levers past 90° of heel, with free trim. Add loading conditions with tanks and free-surface correction, lost-buoyancy damage cases, and the IMO intact and weather criteria as arithmetic on these numbers.

  4. Find where it heels

    Put a weight on deck and SeaCraft finds where the hull settles, with heel, trim and draft solved together at large angles. The weight pulls the centre of gravity G off the centreline, and the hull heels until the centre of buoyancy B is under it. There the righting lever GZ matches the heeling arm the weight gives.

    Each position of the slider is one run of seacraft heel, with nothing interpolated between them.

  5. Estimate the resistance

    Holtrop and Mennen for displacement ships, Michell thin-ship theory, the Delft series for yachts and Savitsky for planing hulls. An optimiser can then adjust Cp, LCB and L/B for a target speed.

  6. See the waves

    Michell’s thin-ship theory gives the wave pattern a hull leaves behind it: the wedge of diverging waves and the transverse waves inside it, changing as the speed rises. These are the free waves far from the hull. The water right beside the hull is not its wave profile.

    Each speed is one run of seacraft waves. The theory fits slender hulls only, so the pattern is shown only for the hulls SeaCraft gives no warning about.

  7. Predict the motions

    Strip-theory heave and pitch in regular head waves, then irregular seas, significant motions and motion sickness incidence.

  8. Hand it on

    A self-contained HTML report collects the measurements. Meshes and curves go out as OBJ, STL, 3MF, PLY, IGES and DXF for CAD and 3D printing. For the workshop there are station moulds, plywood panels and an offsets table, described below.

From the lines to the pieces you cut

SeaCraft draws what goes on the workshop floor. Station moulds, the profile and the transom come out full size, with the thickness of the skin taken off. The table of offsets prints as a PDF. Plywood panels for stitch-and-glue are laid flat with their measurements written on: overall length and width, offsets from a baseline, and the length of each seam.

The panels can then be nested onto plywood sheets, either scarfed end to end into blanks long enough for the longest piece, or cut at butt joints with matching marks. Each sheet gets its own full-size cutting file.

A panel counts as lying flat when its seams drift less than 2 mm and the plywood stretches less than 0.1 %. Those limits are provisional, and have not yet been checked on a boat built from these patterns.

seacraft panels skiff-4m.fef --nest blanks -o panels.svg

    Let an AI agent work on the hull

    seacraft mcp is a Model Context Protocol server. An agent can load a hull, move control points, measure, render a picture and save, using the same code the desktop app runs.

    The same tools reach the newer analyses too: an agent can find where the hull heels under a weight, draw the wave pattern, or export the plywood panels nested onto sheets.

    Point it at a running modeller and you watch the edits happen. That connection stays on your own machine and needs a token by default, and you can limit which folders it reads and writes.

    The repository includes skills that teach an agent to reach design targets, fair a hull and report stability. They tell it to re-measure after each change, and to look at a render instead of guessing from coordinates.

    Work on a file, with edits held until the agent saves:

    claude mcp add seacraft -- seacraft mcp --open hull.fef

    Or drive a modeller you can see:

    seacraft-gui --control 127.0.0.1:7878 --open hull.fef
claude mcp add seacraft -- seacraft mcp --connect 127.0.0.1:7878

    Download

    Ready-made programs for Windows, macOS and Linux are on the latest release. Each one holds the desktop app, the seacraft command and the example hulls. To begin, choose Open an example on the start screen.

    The current version is {{release}}. Anything may change while it is 0.x.

    The Windows and macOS builds are compiled and tested automatically; trying the app by hand has so far been done on Linux. Feedback from any platform is welcome: open an issue and say what worked and what did not.

    • Windows, 64-bitseacraft-*-x86_64-pc-windows-msvc.zip. Unzip it, then double-click seacraft-gui.exe. The program is not signed, so SmartScreen may warn you.
    • Mac with an Apple chipSeaCraft-*-arm64.dmg. Open it and drag SeaCraft to Applications. The app is not notarised, so the first time, allow it in System Settings > Privacy & Security.
    • Mac with an Intel chipSeaCraft-*-x86_64.dmg, with the same steps.
    • LinuxSeaCraft-*-x86_64.AppImage, a Flatpak bundle, or a plain .tar.gz folder.

    Or build it from source

    You need rustup, which installs the pinned Rust version for you.

    Open the app with an example hull:

    git clone https://github.com/havan/SeaCraft
cd SeaCraft
cargo run --release -p seacraft-gui -- --open examples/trawler-22m.fef

    Or measure from the command line:

    cargo run --release --bin seacraft -- hydro examples/trawler-22m.fef --kg 2.5

    On Ubuntu, the desktop app also needs these libraries:

    sudo apt-get install libxkbcommon-dev libwayland-dev libegl1-mesa-dev \
  libgl1-mesa-dev libx11-dev libxcursor-dev libxrandr-dev libxi-dev

    How far the numbers have been checked

    Compared with

    • Tank tests of the Wigley hull. Michell’s wave resistance, against Kajitani et al. (1983), puts the hollow at the measured speed, but the hump comes about 0.02 early in Froude number and 18 to 54 % high. Strip-theory motions, against Journée (1992): in waves at least 1.25 ship lengths long at Froude numbers 0.2 and 0.3, away from resonance, heave on both hulls and pitch on one come within 30 % and 30° of the tank. Shorter waves are further off, and the pitch resonance comes at shorter waves than the tank’s.
    • Worked examples printed with the methods: Holtrop and Mennen (1982) and Holtrop (1984), and Savitsky as Molland et al. (2011) work it. SeaCraft gives the 1984 example to the printed kilonewton and the 1982 one 1.2 % high, from the newer form factor it uses; Savitsky comes within 0.5 %. That checks the transcription of the equations, not that the methods predict any real boat.
    • The Wigley hull’s published equation. The example’s hydrostatics are within 0.6 % of the closed forms at the design draft, but its volume runs 5.5 % low at a quarter of it. Its cross-curve levers KN to 80° are within 0.71 % of an independent integration, at most 2.7 mm.
    • Closed-form cases, such as box barges, including the heel under an off-centre weight.
    • SeaCraft’s own earlier output. The demo hull’s numbers only catch unintended changes, and say nothing about whether they are right.

    Not systematically compared with

    • Tank tests of any hull but the Wigley.
    • Sea trials or other full-scale measurements.
    • Approved naval-architecture software.
